# Naming a project

The name becomes the repo, the domain, the package, the bundle ID and the store listing. It is cheap to choose carefully now and expensive to change once it is in all of those.

## First, say why the working name fails

Most working names are descriptive — `my-life-goals`, `simple-invoice-tool`, `ai-notes-app`. Three problems, worth stating plainly rather than hinting at:

1. **It describes rather than names.** Nothing to trademark, nothing to remember.
2. **Prefixes like `my-` read as software from twenty years ago.**
3. **The descriptive phrase is the most contested one in the category**, so the product is invisible in store and search results from day one.

## Where good names come from

Find the product's central *action* or *shape*, then look for a metaphor that already carries it. The best names encode the concept without stating it.

Useful families:

| Family | Carries | Examples of the shape |
|---|---|---|
| Navigation | Direction, distance, progress along a path | markers, stars, bearings, waypoints |
| Cultivation | Growth, tending, patience, return | groves, seasons, perennials |
| Record | Accumulation, the long view, history | almanacs, ledgers, chronicles |
| Structure | Assembly from parts, load-bearing pieces | keystones, scaffolds, foundations |

Avoid coined SaaS names — `Goalio`, `Taskly`, `Strivo`. They are available precisely because they carry nothing.

**The test worth applying:** does the name carry *more than one* thing the product does? A name meaning both "built one piece at a time" and "left behind for others to follow" is doing two jobs in one word. That is rare and worth holding out for.

## Check availability in the right order

Order matters, because the cheapest thing to work around should be checked last:

1. **App Store and Play Store**, if there will ever be an app. A store name collision is the one that actually blocks you.
2. **Trademark**, in the relevant class. Collisions in unrelated industries are usually survivable; collisions in software are not.
3. **Domain**, last. `.com` is rarely available and rarely necessary now — `.app`, `.so`, `.io` are all fine.

**A web search is not clearance.** Say so. Report what you find as encouraging or discouraging, and tell the human to check the stores and a trademark database directly before spending money.

## After choosing

- **Repo name, brand and app name should be identical.** Lowercase for the repo. No `-app` suffix.
- **Write a decision record** covering the alternatives considered and why they lost. In four months someone will suggest one of the rejected names again.
- **Note what the metaphor gives you.** A name with a strong image often hands you the icon, the loading state, the progress visualisation and the vocabulary for free — and that vocabulary should go straight into the glossary.

## Watch for

**Names worn out by product vocabulary.** "North Star" is a good metaphor destroyed by a decade of "north star metric". Same for "compass", "lighthouse", "beacon". Check whether the phrase has become jargon before falling for the image.

**Singular names for plural products.** A guiding-star name for something holding many equal items is subtly wrong, and people feel it without being able to say why.

**Words the target user might not know.** A beautiful obscure word makes people hesitate, and hesitation at the moment of first contact is expensive. Weigh it honestly rather than choosing the prettier option by default.
